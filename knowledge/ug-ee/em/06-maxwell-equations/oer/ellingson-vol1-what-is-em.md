Short excerpt from Ellingson, *Electromagnetics*, Volume 1, Section 1.1 “What is Electromagnetics?” Licence CC BY-SA 4.0. Downstream notes in this pack are original; this file is the only copied paragraph.

The topic of this book is applied engineering electromagnetics. This topic is often described as “the theory of electromagnetic fields and waves,” which is both true and misleading. The truth is that electric fields, magnetic fields, their sources, waves, and the behavior these waves are all topics covered by this book. The misleading part is that our principal aim shall be to close the gap between basic electrical circuit theory and the more general theory that is required to address certain topics that are of broad and common interest in the field of electrical engineering.

Applied engineering electromagnetics is the study of those aspects of electrical engineering in situations in which the electromagnetic properties of materials and the geometry in which those materials are arranged is important. This requires an understanding of electromagnetic fields and waves, which are of primary interest in some applications.
